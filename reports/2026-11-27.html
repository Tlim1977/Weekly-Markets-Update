Executive view

The US market's four-session Thanksgiving week began with a sharp Monday rebound, gave back some ground on Tuesday and Wednesday, then finished strongly on Friday's shortened session. The S&P 500 gained 5.6%, its strongest weekly advance since April 2025. The Nasdaq rose 6.7%, reflecting renewed buying in large technology and semiconductor names. The rally followed a bruising prior week and does not, by itself, establish that the correction risk has passed.

Singapore's benchmark gained 0.4% across five sessions. Friday's 0.66% rise took the index to 4,995.23, but it remains well below its recent highs. Breadth on Friday was positive, with 418 gainers against 166 decliners, and S$1.6 billion of securities traded. Seatrium remained a notable weak spot after announcing a workforce redesign affecting about 220 Singapore employees.

What drove the move? The rebound was broad enough to lift all four major indices, with the Nasdaq outperforming. Technology and semiconductor names helped lead Friday's advance, including Broadcom and Micron; Amazon also rallied as the holiday shopping period began. The market's focus remains on whether the enormous investment cycle in AI infrastructure can translate into durable earnings and cash flows. That valuation debate has been a source of sharp swings rather than a settled bullish thesis.

Volatility eased, but stayed elevated

VIX ended Friday around 24, down sharply from Wednesday's level. A retreat in implied volatility is consistent with a relief rally, but a reading in the mid-20s still signals meaningful demand for protection.

Rates remain a watchpoint

The US 10-year Treasury yield was around 4.93% at Friday's close. A rise in yields alongside equities can indicate stronger growth expectations, but it can also constrain valuation multiples if it persists.

The STI recovered modestly over the week but remained below 5,000. Friday's advance was supported by broad positive breadth. UOL was among the stronger blue chips, while Seatrium remained the weakest STI constituent on the day. The index's weekly gain was small compared with the US rebound, underlining the more defensive and domestically weighted nature of the Singapore market.

Corporate and macro developments

  • Seatrium: announced a workforce redesign expected to reduce its Singapore workforce by about 2%, or roughly 220 roles, as it consolidates its global yard network. The company said it does not expect the exercise to disrupt ongoing operations. The market will watch whether restructuring improves execution and margins without impairing delivery capacity.
  • Singtel: its FY2027 interim dividend is S$0.09 per share, comprising S$0.071 core and S$0.019 value-realisation components. The ex-date is 30 November, record date 1 December and payment date 11 December. Investors buying on or after the ex-date will not receive this distribution.
  • Singapore growth: advance third-quarter GDP showed 6.1% year-on-year growth; the 2026 growth forecast was raised to around 6.0%, while the 2027 projection was 2.5%–4.5%. October CPI rose 1.9% year-on-year and fell 0.3% month-on-month. October industrial production increased 7.1% month-on-month on a seasonally adjusted basis.

Investor takeaway

The US rebound was impressive, but it followed a sharp sell-off and arrived in a holiday-shortened week. Investors should distinguish a fast relief rally from a durable improvement in earnings expectations, market breadth and liquidity. Keep an eye on Friday's US employment report and the early-week manufacturing and services surveys.

Singapore's market stabilised, but the STI's return to just under 5,000 is not yet a decisive recovery. Bank shares rose on Friday, while Seatrium's restructuring and Singtel's dividend timetable are useful reminders that company-specific developments can dominate index direction. For income investors, note the Singtel ex-date rather than treating the dividend as an automatic total-return gain.

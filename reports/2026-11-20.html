Executive view

The week was dominated by a violent de-rating of AI and semiconductor shares. The S&P 500 and Dow entered correction territory, the Nasdaq erased its 2026 gain, and the STI fell 4.9% for the week. Treasury yields retreated as investors sought safety, even as hawkish Fed commentary kept December rate-hike expectations elevated.

What drove the sell-off

  • AI valuation and spending doubts: Anthropic’s high-profile IPO failed to restore confidence. Its shares fell 9.1% on the second trading day, while Nvidia dropped 13.8% despite a very strong quarterly report. Investors questioned whether extraordinary AI infrastructure spending can earn an adequate return quickly enough.
  • Semiconductors and AI infrastructure were hit hardest: The weakness spread from Nvidia to chipmakers, cloud infrastructure and data-centre suppliers. The Nasdaq fell 10% for the week, while the Philadelphia semiconductor index’s year-to-date gain had narrowed sharply.
  • Fed and inflation concerns: Governor Lisa Cook argued that rates remain too low to bring inflation down and that further increases may be required. Reuters reported markets were pricing in a near-certain 25bp hike at the December 8–9 meeting.
  • Company-specific pressure: Intuit fell 12.8% after lowering its annual revenue-growth forecast and agreeing to sell Mailchimp. Caterpillar fell about 5% amid concern that weaker AI-related data-centre demand could affect its power-generation equipment business.

Reuters reported declining shares outnumbered advancers by 3.77 to 1 on the NYSE and 4.27 to 1 on Nasdaq. US exchange volume was 25.19 billion shares versus a 20-session average of 19.50 billion. The VIX closed at its highest level since 7 April 2025. These are signs of broad de-risking rather than a routine rotation in a handful of mega-cap names. Reuters: Friday US market close.

Macro, inflation and rates

Inflation

October CPI rose 0.4% month on month, after +0.6% in September. Core CPI rose 0.3% for a third consecutive month. Food rose 0.5% and energy 0.7%.

Important caveat: BLS could not publish most October 2026 year-on-year CPI changes because October 2025 survey data were not collected during the federal funding lapse. Avoid treating a missing annual figure as zero. BLS October CPI.

Employment

October payrolls increased by 165,000; unemployment was 4.1%. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% month on month and 3.1% year on year.

The labour market remains resilient enough to complicate the case for rate cuts. BLS October employment report.

US Treasury par yields fell on Friday: the 2-year ended at 4.63% (−25bp for the week) and the 10-year at 4.92% (−23bp). That rally is consistent with safe-haven demand and concern about the growth implications of the equity rout, even as inflation and Fed rhetoric remain hawkish. Treasury par-yield data and weekly changes.

Consumer sentiment improved to 51.5 in November from 48.0 in October, but remained weak by historical standards. University of Michigan survey coverage.

AI and financial-stability angle

Beyond stock valuations, Fed Governor Michael Barr warned that AI could amplify cyber, operational and market risks. In the Fed’s latest Financial Stability Report survey, 87% of respondents identified AI as a salient financial-stability risk, including the possibility that an AI earnings slowdown could trigger wider equity losses. Reuters on Barr’s remarks.

REITs, trusts and distributions

  • Singapore REITs were not immune to the sell-off. On Wednesday, 18 November, 29 of 38 S-REITs fell; Mapletree Industrial Trust was reported down 4.05% that session. Midweek REIT market recap.
  • Distribution dates matter: Frasers Logistics & Commercial Trust and NTT DC REIT both went ex-distribution on 18 November. SGX records show payment dates of 23 December for FLCT and 18 December for NTT DC REIT. Price drops around an ex-date may partly reflect the distribution leaving the unit price; do not read the whole move as a change in operating value. FLCT SGX record · NTT DC REIT SGX record.
  • For income-oriented trusts, the key cross-currents remain funding costs, refinancing spreads, occupancy and rental reversions. The global rise in rate expectations is a sector headwind even when local property fundamentals are stable.

Singapore macro and MAS

  • Growth: Advance estimates showed 3Q2026 GDP growth of 5.4% year on year, moderating from 6.2% in 2Q. The 2026 GDP growth forecast had previously been raised to 4.5%–5.5%. The full 3Q Economic Survey is due on 24 November.
  • Trade: October non-oil domestic exports expanded 79.8% year on year, following 68.3% growth in September. The 3Q wholesale-trade series showed domestic sales up 19.5% and foreign sales up 38.2% year on year.
  • Inflation and policy: September headline CPI inflation was 2.2% year on year. MAS kept the S$NEER policy-band slope unchanged in October and projected 2026 core inflation at 1.5%–2.0%, with headline inflation around 2.0%. It expects inflation to ease more clearly from mid-2027.

Potential risks — not scheduled events

  • AI capex and monetisation: Watch whether management commentary from HPE, Dell and chip suppliers supports or undermines the expected return on data-centre spending.
  • US policy and export controls: Further debate over AI infrastructure, data-centre construction and advanced-chip exports could affect semiconductor and cloud-equipment valuations. No specific new policy announcement is confirmed in the calendar reviewed.
  • Geopolitics and energy: Middle East developments could change oil prices and inflation expectations. This remains a risk scenario, not a scheduled catalyst.
  • Fed repricing: The December 8–9 meeting is the next policy decision. The market may react sharply if PCE and the Beige Book reinforce the case for tighter policy.

4. Overall investor takeaway

  • Separate the AI valuation shock from the economic data. Strong recent payroll growth and ongoing AI demand do not remove the risk that valuations and capital spending expectations had become stretched.
  • Rates remain a key transmission channel. Falling Treasury yields this week offered some relief, but the Fed’s hawkish stance and upcoming PCE data could quickly reverse that move. Rate-sensitive REITs and long-duration equities remain exposed.
  • Singapore is more defensive, not insulated. Banks, industrial names and REITs were sold alongside regional markets. The STI’s positive YTD return is still intact, but its short-term trend has weakened materially.
  • Use the holiday week carefully. The US has only three full sessions plus a half-day. Earnings, PCE and Treasury auctions are concentrated into a few sessions, so liquidity may be thinner and price gaps more pronounced.

Bottom line: The next test is whether the AI sell-off stabilises while inflation and earnings data remain consistent with resilient growth. Until that becomes clearer, volatility and stock-specific dispersion are likely to remain elevated.

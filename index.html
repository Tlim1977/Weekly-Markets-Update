How market scores are calculated

Each market receives a directional score from −100 to +100. The score is translated into a plain-English market condition.

IndicatorWeightWhat it measures
Index trend35%Position versus 50-day and 200-day moving averages
Market breadth30%How widely gains or losses are shared across stocks
Sector performance20%How many sectors are participating in the move
Momentum15%Recent price strength and direction

Score guide: +40 to +100 Strongly bullish; +15 to +39 Bullish; −14 to +14 Neutral; −15 to −39 Bearish; −40 to −100 Strongly bearish.

For Singapore, the sector component is omitted and the remaining index-trend, breadth and momentum weights are proportionally adjusted to total 100%. Volatility is assessed separately. High volatility means larger expected or observed price swings, not necessarily falling prices.

The score will remain unavailable until all required indicator feeds are connected and validated.

🇺🇸 US volatility guide

Uses the CBOE VIX, which reflects options-market expectations for S&P 500 volatility over approximately the next 30 days.

LevelVIXMeaning
CalmBelow 15Relatively settled conditions
Normal15–20Typical market fluctuations
Elevated20–30Greater uncertainty and larger swings
High StressAbove 30Significant uncertainty and sharp moves possible

These are practical guideposts, not official market boundaries.

🇸🇬 Singapore volatility guide

Uses the Straits Times Index's 20-day realised volatility, calculated from its daily returns and annualised. This measures how much the index has actually fluctuated.

Calm, Normal, Elevated and High Stress bands use Singapore-specific historical thresholds derived from the trailing five years of STI volatility data.

The US VIX thresholds are not reused for Singapore because the two measurements are different.
